# Literature: stopping over-reliance on an auxiliary lookup (ICE)

All arXiv ids below were opened on 26 Oct 2026 and the titles/authors matched. Summaries come from abstracts only. I did not read full texts, so mechanism details beyond the abstract are from my background knowledge and marked "(bg)".

## Diagnosis in one line
ICE-dropout (20% whole-sequence "no match") only teaches "lookup absent". It never teaches "lookup present but wrong". Wrong-lookup tokens carry a large share of the loss, and the model is never trained to disagree with the table. The model also builds the backbone around ICE, which explains why C_off is worse than B. The literature calls the second failure modality laziness / greedy learning. The first is the RAG distractor problem.

## A. Training with misleading context (the missing "wrong lookup" training signal)
1. **Making Retrieval-Augmented Language Models Robust to Irrelevant Context** (Yoran, Wolfson, Ram, Berant, 2023) arXiv:2310.01558. Fine-tuning on a mix of relevant and irrelevant retrieved contexts makes the model ignore distractors without losing the gain from good retrieval. Change: during training, replace the true longest-match successor with a wrong one (a different occurrence's successor, or a random token drawn from the n-gram's other successors) in X% of positions, with the true target unchanged. Per-token corruption is better than per-sequence.
2. **RAFT: Adapting Language Model to Domain Specific RAG** (Zhang et al., 2024) arXiv:2403.10131. Trains with some examples that contain only distractor documents, so the model learns to disregard them. Change: use a mixture of three position types: lookup correct, lookup corrupted, and lookup absent (the current dropout). Keep it per-token.
3. **The Power of Noise** (Cuconasu et al., 2024) arXiv:2401.14887. Distractors that look relevant hurt most, while random documents can even help. Change: build hard negatives for ICE, meaning the same n-gram with a stale or other-object continuation (the conflict_g5 and override_diff cases). Random corruption is the weaker baseline.
4. **Entity-Based Knowledge Conflicts in QA** (Longpre et al., 2021) arXiv:2109.05052. Models over-rely on memorised facts. Training with substituted-entity counterfactuals reduces this. Change: a swap-successor augmentation, the same idea as item 1 on the ICE side.
5. **Adaptive Chameleon or Stubborn Sloth** (Xie et al., 2023) arXiv:2305.13300. Analyses how LLMs weigh counter-evidence against their own knowledge. Change: use it only to design eval, i.e. a table that reports follow-wrong rate against the model's own confidence.
6. **Knowledge Conflicts for LLMs: A Survey** (Xu et al., 2024) arXiv:2403.08319. Taxonomy of context-memory and inter-context conflicts and their mitigations. Change: citation for the "two occurrences disagree" case (inter-context conflict) in the paper's framing.

## B. Learned verification, abstention, critique
7. **Self-RAG** (Asai et al., 2023) arXiv:2310.11511. Trains the model to emit reflection tokens that judge whether retrieval is needed and whether the output is supported. Change: add an auxiliary head that predicts "is the longest-match successor correct" (binary, supervised from the data for free). Feed its output (stop-gradient or not) into the logit-bonus gate.
8. **Corrective RAG (CRAG)** (Yan et al., 2024) arXiv:2401.15884. A lightweight evaluator grades retrieved items and filters weak ones before use. Change: the same "verifier before use" structure. The verifier head sees the backbone hidden state (so it can reason about content) plus the match features, not only the counts.
9. **Chain-of-Note** (Yu et al., 2023) arXiv:2311.09210. The model writes notes assessing each retrieved document before answering, which improves robustness to noise. Change: only conceptual support. It needs generation, so it is too expensive for ICE.
10. **SelectiveNet** (Geifman and El-Yaniv, 2019) arXiv:1901.09192. Trains the predictor and a selection head jointly under a coverage constraint. Change: train the gate with a risk-coverage objective, i.e. penalise using ICE where it hurts, with a coverage target for how often it fires.

## C. Copy / generate gating
11. **Get To The Point: Pointer-Generator Networks** (See, Liu, Manning, 2017) arXiv:1704.04368. A soft gate p_gen mixes a copy distribution and a generation distribution. Coverage penalises repeated copying. Change: replace the additive logit bonus with a mixture, p = g*onehot(s*) + (1-g)*p_LM, with g computed from backbone state. The mixture makes the cost of a wrong copy explicit in the loss. Additive biases can drift upward without bound.
12. **Pointer Sentinel Mixture Models** (Merity et al., 2016) arXiv:1609.07843. A sentinel lets the model place mass on "none of the copy candidates". Change: the same as item 11, with g conditioned on the hidden state and not only on counts.
13. **Improving Neural LMs with a Continuous Cache** (Grave, Joulin, Usunier, 2016) arXiv:1612.04426. Interpolates a cache over recent hidden states with a tuned lambda. The simplest baseline, and the closest in spirit to ICE. Change: cite as the baseline for a fixed-lambda, count-only trust.
14. **In-context Learning and Induction Heads** (Olsson et al., 2022) arXiv:2209.11895. Induction heads implement "previous occurrence, copy its successor", the same thing ICE does with a table. Change: framing only. ICE is an external induction head, so the gate has to learn what induction heads learn implicitly about when to copy.

## D. kNN-LM and adaptive interpolation
15. **Generalization through Memorization (kNN-LM)** (Khandelwal et al., 2019) arXiv:1911.00172. Fixed-lambda interpolation of an LM and a kNN distribution. Change: baseline. A fixed lambda is what C-style bonuses reduce to.
16. **Adaptive Semiparametric Language Models** (Yogatama, de Masson d'Autume, Kong, 2021) arXiv:2102.02557. A learned gate chooses between local context, short-term cache and long-term memory per prediction, from the hidden state. Change: gate on the hidden state (content), not only on count features. This is likely why agree/rel barely helped: they are weak predictors compared with the LM's own state.
17. **You can't pick your neighbors, or can you? When and how to rely on retrieval in kNN-LM** (Drozdov et al., 2022) arXiv:2210.15859. Shows that overlap between the retrieved items and the query drives kNN-LM quality, and sets lambda from retrieval quality (about 4% perplexity gain). Change: add a "similarity between current context and the context of the matched position" feature. Compare the preceding tokens beyond n (e.g. the match length extended up to 32, or a hash of the tokens before the match). ICE's longest-match length is partly this already, so verify with an ablation.
18. **Efficient Nearest Neighbor Language Models** (He, Neubig, Berg-Kirkpatrick, 2021) arXiv:2109.04212. Includes adaptive retrieval, skipping the lookup when the LM is confident. Change: let the backbone's own entropy gate the output bonus (ICE helps most where B is uncertain).
19. **RetoMaton** (Alon et al., 2022) arXiv:2201.12431. Reduces lookups by following automaton pointers. Relevance is low. It is about cost, not trust.

## E. Modality laziness and imbalance (why C_off < B)
20. **What Makes Training Multi-Modal Classification Networks Hard?** (Wang, Tran, Feiszli, 2019) arXiv:1905.12681. Different inputs overfit and learn at different rates. Gradient Blending reweights them with auxiliary single-modality losses. Change: add an auxiliary loss on the ICE-free path, i.e. a second head or a second forward with ICE masked, and weight it. This directly attacks "C_off worse than B". Costs about 2x forward on the masked loss, or a cheaper version where the mask is applied for a sampled 20-30% of batches (already close to what dropout does, but with the loss on the same tokens).
21. **Balanced Multimodal Learning via On-the-fly Gradient Modulation (OGM-GE)** (Peng et al., 2022) arXiv:2203.15332. Slows the gradient of the dominant modality and adds noise to it. Change: scale the gradient into the ICEInput and ICEOutput parameters by a ratio of ICE-path to backbone contribution (a measure like the loss gap between ICE-on and ICE-off). Cheap to implement but needs tuning.
22. **Characterizing and overcoming the greedy nature of learning in multi-modal DNNs** (Wu, Jastrzebski, Cho, Geras, 2022) arXiv:2202.05306. Proposes a conditional-utilisation-rate metric and a rebalancing method that trains the under-used modality. Change: report "utilisation of the backbone vs ICE" as a metric in the paper, which supports the over-reliance story, and use it as the rebalancing weight.
23. **FractalNet** (Larsson, Maire, Shakhnarovich, 2016) arXiv:1605.07648. Introduced drop-path (randomly dropping branches). Change: only the origin of path dropout. Relevant knob: make the dropout per-token and per-order instead of per-sequence, and anneal the rate.

## F. Speculative-decoding connection
24. **Fast Inference from Transformers via Speculative Decoding** (Leviathan, Kalman, Matias, 2022) arXiv:2211.17192. A draft token is accepted with probability min(1, p_target/p_draft). (bg for formula.) Change: use acceptance as a training signal. The label for the verifier head is "would the backbone have accepted s*", or just whether s* equals the target. A principled alternative to item 7. Also gives an inference-time safety: reject s* when the backbone's own probability for s* is very low.

## Most promising, cheapest fixes (ranked)
1. **Per-token successor corruption in training** (items 1, 2, 4). Small change in the data loader, which already computes features on CPU. Corrupt the successor and match features consistently in about 10-20% of matched positions (replace by another occurrence's successor or a random token). This is the only fix that supplies the missing "present but wrong" signal. It needs no architectural change and costs one arm. Falsifiable: follow-wrong rate should fall toward B's 0.15-0.20 while copyable loss stays near C.
2. **Auxiliary "lookup is correct" head plus hidden-state-conditioned gate** (items 7, 8, 16, 24). The label is free (s* == target). It replaces count-only trust with content-aware trust, the likely reason agree/rel did nothing. Fits ICEOutput. Cheap: one linear layer, one BCE loss, with the bonus scaled by its probability. Risk: the head has to see enough context. Put it at a late layer.
3. **Mixture output instead of additive logit bonus** (items 11, 12). p = g*onehot(s*) + (1-g)*p_LM. A wrong copy now costs -log(1-g), so the loss itself punishes overconfidence. Small code change. Combine with 1 and 2.
4. **ICE-off auxiliary loss on a sampled fraction of batches, weighted** (item 20, also 22). Targets C_off < B. Compute the LM loss with ICE fully masked on about 25% of batches (token-level mask, not only whole-sequence), so the backbone cannot offload copying. Almost free beyond what ICE-dropout already does. The difference from ICE-dropout is that it is the same tokens, and the weight can be raised.
5. (Optional) **Context-similarity feature beyond the n-gram** (item 17). Add the extended match length and the previous-token agreement as inputs to the verifier. Check whether it is redundant with match-length buckets before spending a run.

Suggested run plan within 5-10 arms: C+corrupt, C+corrupt+verifier head, C+mixture+corrupt+verifier. Compare follow-wrong, lookup-wrong loss, C_off and CodeRecall override/conflict cases against B and C.

## Caveats
- None of these papers uses an exact n-gram table with a linear-recurrent backbone, so transfer is by analogy. The RAG papers are about prompts and large models, not 140M pretraining.
- No published result shows that gradient-modulation methods fix lookup-style shortcuts in LMs. Treat 20-22 as less certain than 1-3.
- I did not find a paper that supervises the copy gate with "was the lookup right" labels in a pretrained LM, beyond the generic supervised-gate and verifier ideas above. This may be the novelty claim, but I only did a limited search.
