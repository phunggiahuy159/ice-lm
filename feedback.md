"Overall
I’m curious and excited whether this works. The idea is interesting: if a cheap lookup can handle some exact copying, we might be able to reduce attention without losing much quality. It’s worth trying.

Main concern
Even verbatim retrieval can be tricky. The same phrase may appear with different continuations, or the relevant information may be requested using different wording. How reliably can the model choose the right occurrence and ignore a misleading lookup?
I’d also watch for overreliance on copying. Better exact recall doesn’t necessarily mean better reasoning, especially when the model needs to combine information or revise an earlier value.

Baselines and metrics
The attention-ratio comparisons and ablations are sensible. I’d emphasize actual total memory and speed, including the lookup table and its overhead, alongside the quality change. How much are we saving, and what performance are we giving up?

What to improve
Pick one concrete use case first such as copying identifiers or configuration values in code and show where this helps. Include repeated names with conflicting values, corrections later in the context, and cases where copying is the wrong behavior.
A clear saving on a realistic workload, with a small and understood performance tradeoff, would be a convincing result."
